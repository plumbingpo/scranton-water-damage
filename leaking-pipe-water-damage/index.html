Dealing With Leaking Pipe Water Damage in Scranton

A leaking pipe rarely announces itself. Water seeps out behind drywall, at the back of a sink cabinet or under the concrete until a stain, a soft floor or an unexplained water bill gives it away. By then the materials around the pipe have often been wet for weeks. We serve Scranton homeowners by tracing the full wet area and drying every bit of it, including what is out of sight.

A plumber can stop the drip, though the water that soaked into the structure is still there afterward. This is where our team comes in. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map the wet materials, open only what has to be opened, and set air movers and dehumidifiers until readings are back to normal. A slow leak gives mold plenty of time to begin, so we inspect for growth too and treat any surface it has reached.

Do This First

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut Off the WaterWhen a toilet or sink line is dripping, shut the valve on the supply right at that fixture. When the source is unclear, the house main is the valve to turn off.
  2. Stay Clear of ElectricityTurn off the breaker serving the area when water is close to a light fixture, switch or outlet. Do not touch wet electrical devices or cords.
  3. Check the Water MeterWith every tap off, look at the meter. If the dial is still moving, a pipe is leaking somewhere you cannot see, possibly under the slab.
  4. Empty and PhotographClear out the cabinet or closet near the leak and take photos of stains, warped materials and the pipe itself before anything is repaired or thrown away.
  5. Have the Area MeteredBefore the opening is sealed, get the surrounding materials checked with a moisture meter. Drywall can feel dry to the touch while the cavity behind it is still damp.
Will Insurance Pay?

What About Insurance?

A slow leak and a sudden one are not handled the same way by insurance. Coverage is common for accidental, sudden damage, while a leak that ran for months or weeks is usually not covered and is specifically excluded in some policies. A hidden leak you had no way of knowing about may be handled more favorably than one that was visible and ignored. Your agent and the policy itself are the places to confirm. Our team records where the leak came from, along with photos and moisture readings, to give the adjuster clear facts.

Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • Leaks at a sink drain joint or trap, which release a little water with every use.
  • A crimp ring or solder joint that gives out inside a ceiling or wall cavity where nobody can see it.
  • Loose or worn compression fittings and supply connections under sinks and behind toilets.
  • A hairline split left by freezing weather in a line running through a crawl space or outside wall.
  • Fasteners from remodeling work or wall hangings that nick or pierce a line inside the wall.
If It Is Left

Where the Damage Spreads

Flooring Damage

Moisture from a leaking slab or wall line spreads out below the finished floor. Laminate seams swell, hardwood boards cup, and the glue holding tile or vinyl releases.

Soaked Wall Insulation

Like a sponge, wall insulation takes in water from the pipe and keeps the framing next to it damp. Left in a sealed cavity it can stay wet for a long time, which is why we usually take it out.

Rotted Framing

Wall studs and bottom plates that stay damp for weeks begin to soften and decay. Once framing lumber is no longer sound, it must be removed and new wood put in.

Stained Drywall and Ceilings

A pipe leaking above a ceiling or inside a wall leaves brown rings and bubbled paint. Drywall that stays wet goes soft, sags and may need to be cut out.

How We Handle Leaking Pipe Water Damage

1

Find Every Wet Area

Every wet area gets marked after a scan of the walls, cabinets and floors with meters and thermal imaging, and that includes places water followed the framing to, some distance from the leak.

2

Open What Is Needed

Small, planned openings give us access to wet cavities. Insulation, drywall or cabinet pieces beyond saving come out, and moldy material is bagged to keep spores from spreading through the house.

3

Dry the Structure

We keep dehumidifiers and air movers going until the subfloor and framing read normal, which usually takes 3 to 5 days. We check readings on each visit and adjust the equipment as materials dry.

4

Treat and Document

Once the surfaces involved are treated with an antimicrobial, we hand over our photos and moisture logs. That paperwork proves the area was dry ahead of repairs and can support a claim.
