Of all the problems an owner can have, sewage coming up through the drains ranks among the most dangerous. Raw sewage contains dangerous bacteria, viruses, parasites and other pathogens, including E. coli, hepatitis, salmonella and giardia, that pose serious health risks to anyone who touches contaminated materials. When it happens in Scranton, Scranton Restoration is available 24/7 with technicians trained for this work, proper protective gear and disinfectants registered with the EPA, everything needed for a safe decontamination.

The industry classifies sewage as black water, or Category 3, which is the most dangerous and most contaminated type of water damage there is. Because of it, cleanup follows the toughest rules in the trade, which means full protective gear, barriers to contain the area, tearing out any porous material that met sewage and disinfecting all that is left. Our technicians follow IICRC S500 standards for Category 3 water damage so your property is restored to safe occupancy standards.

The cleanup process involves removing all standing sewage, extracting contaminated water from structural materials, removing and properly disposing of every porous material that contacted sewage (carpet, padding, drywall, insulation), thoroughly disinfecting hard surfaces, applying antimicrobial treatments and completing structural drying to prevent secondary mold growth. Lingering sewage odor is eliminated with thermal fogging and industrial hydroxyl generators, which we run in the affected area.

Signs You Need Sewage Cleanup in Scranton

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Every sink, tub and shower draining slowly even after you plunge them
  • Wet spots on floors or walls accompanied by foul odors
  • Drain openings or basement slabs coated with visible sewage residue
  • Soggy, smelly ground over the sewer line or leakage at the cleanout cap
  • Unexplained illness or gastrointestinal symptoms among household members
  • Toilets or drains that gurgle whenever you run water somewhere else
  • Sewage or dark water backing up through floor drains, toilets or tubs

Your Sewage Cleanup Questions Answered

Sewage backups are extremely dangerous. The viruses, parasites and bacteria in raw sewage can lead to E. coli infection, respiratory disease, hepatitis and other serious illness. Do not try cleaning it up unless you have proper protective equipment. Call (708) 391-5296 for professional cleanup right away.
Most backups start with a blockage in the home's sewer line. Typical culprits are tree roots entering the pipe, grease hardening inside it and wipes or similar items flushed down a toilet. Other possibilities are a failed old pipe and a municipal sewer overloaded by heavy rain, which sends sewage up through floor drains. To know for certain, have the line camera-inspected once cleanup is finished.
First, get everyone, animals included, away from the contaminated rooms. Stop using sinks, toilets and appliances that drain, since more water makes the backup worse. Turn off power and the HVAC system to the affected area only if you can do so without touching contaminated water. Leave the cleanup alone, and wash well with soap if any got on your skin.

Why Hire a Professional for Sewage Cleanup?

Sewage cleanup is not something any homeowner should attempt. The biological hazards in raw sewage require specialized training, proper personal protective equipment (Tyvek suits, respirators, protective gloves and boots) and EPA-registered disinfection products that are not available to consumers. Hepatitis, gastrointestinal disease and infections of the skin or respiratory system are among the serious illnesses these pathogens cause.

Professional sewage cleanup also requires proper disposal of contaminated materials as biohazard waste. Putting carpet, insulation or drywall soaked with sewage out with household trash breaks the law and endangers sanitation workers and your community. Licensed biohazard disposal facilities take this waste from certified restoration firms, which keep the required protocols in place.

How to Prevent Sewage Cleanup Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Know the location and condition of your sewer cleanout for emergency access
  • Protect against municipal sewer backups by having a backwater valve fitted where your line connects
  • Food waste, oils and grease block pipes, so put them in the trash and not the sink
  • Have your sewer line inspected with a camera every 2 to 3 years to catch potential problems
  • Use drain strainers in showers and sinks to catch hair and debris
  • Stormwater from a sump or downspout should never drain into the sewer, since the extra volume invites a backup
