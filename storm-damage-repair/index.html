How to Prevent Storm Damage Repair Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Install impact-resistant windows or storm shutters if you live in a hurricane-prone area
  • Schedule a roof inspection every year so weak spots are repaired before storm season
  • Take dated photos of the house every year, since insurers ask about prior condition
  • Make sure the attic is properly ventilated and insulated to prevent ice dams
  • Cracked caulk lets blown rain in, so inspect and renew the seals on doors and windows
  • Trim trees near your home to lower the risk of wind-driven branch damage

Why Professional Storm Damage Repair Matters

Storm damage creates complex, connected repair needs that take experienced restoration professionals to handle properly. What seems like a small leak indoors can hide widespread water damage in wall cavities, roof decking and insulation, which turns into mold and structural decay unless it is identified and dried properly. Our technicians trace where the water traveled using thermal imaging plus moisture meters, so no affected area is overlooked.

Emergency board-up and tarping also take proper technique to be effective. If a tarp is fastened poorly, it can direct water where you do not want it or give out when the wind picks up. We have done a lot of storm mitigation, and we use suitable materials and methods so the temporary protection holds until permanent repairs are on the calendar.

When to Call for Storm Damage Repair in Scranton

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • After a weather event, insulation inside walls or overhead feels soaked
  • Gutters or downspouts torn loose, bent or dumping water against the house
  • Ceilings or walls that stain while it is storming or soon afterward
  • Bubbling paint and soft, drooping spots overhead once the rain stops
  • A musty smell indoors in the days after heavy rain
  • From ground level, the roof shows shingles torn off, broken or raised
  • Standing water in attic spaces, especially by skylights and chimneys
