Dealing With Appliance Leak Water Damage in Scranton

Three appliances cause most of the leaks we see. A washing machine supply hose can split and run at full pressure until someone shuts it off. With a dishwasher the leak is typically slow, coming from the door seal or the drain hose where the cabinets hide it. The ice maker line on a refrigerator is a thin tube, and it can drip for months behind the unit. Each one needs a different kind of cleanup.

For homeowners in Scranton, we begin by sliding the machine out to see how far the water traveled. For a kitchen, the usual places are below the flooring and under the cabinets. With a laundry leak we look at the rooms next to it and the wall behind the washer, plus the ceiling below when the laundry sits on an upper floor. Then our team removes the standing water, meters the materials that soaked it up, and places equipment matched to the size of the damage.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Turn Off the ApplianceStop the cycle and unplug the machine if the outlet and floor around it are dry. If not, switch it off at the breaker instead.
  2. Close the Supply ValveWashers have hot and cold valves on the wall behind them. Look below the sink for the valve that feeds a dishwasher. Refrigerator lines have a small valve behind or below.
  3. Mop Up What You CanMop or towel up what you can reach so it stops spreading under cabinets and flooring. Use a vacuum only if it is a wet and dry model.
  4. Leave the Door ShutA dishwasher or washer that still holds water should stay closed. When the door opens mid-cycle, the full load of water can pour out on the floor.
  5. Photograph the Failed PartKeep the part that failed, whether a hose, a fitting or a valve, and get photos of it. It may be requested by the manufacturer of the appliance and by your insurance company.
Coverage

What About Insurance?

Homeowners policies often cover sudden and accidental events, and a burst hose or a machine overflowing with no warning is that kind of event. Payment typically goes toward the damage the water did, and the appliance or the part that failed is not included. Slow seepage from a dishwasher or ice maker line is a harder claim, because gradual leaks usually are not covered. Certain insurers will want to know the hose's age and whether it was kept up. Check your policy, keep the failed part and take photos before cleanup.

Pricing

What Affects the Cost

  • Removal of cabinets or flooring, compared with drying them in place when that is possible.
  • How long the leak ran before it was found, especially with a slow drip behind an appliance.
  • Spread of the water into other rooms, and into the ceiling beneath an upstairs laundry if there is one.
  • Any mold growth, because it must be dealt with ahead of drying.

Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • Standpipes that clog, or the drain hose from a washer coming loose from one.
  • The water inlet valve on a washer or dishwasher failing to close, which lets the machine overfill.
  • Ice maker lines, copper or plastic, that crack, kink or come loose where they connect.
  • Rubber washing machine supply hoses that weaken with age and split under pressure.
  • Poor installation, such as a dishwasher set out of level or a supply fitting not fully tightened.

What Happens if You Wait

Flooring and Subfloor

A leak runs under laminate, hardwood or vinyl plank flooring with little resistance. Boards cup or swell, and the subfloor underneath stays wet long after the surface looks dry.

Ceiling Below an Upstairs Laundry

Water from a second floor laundry room drops through the floor and collects on the ceiling below. The drywall stains and sags, and the insulation above it stays wet.

Cabinet Damage

Dishwasher leaks soak cabinet sides and toe kicks from underneath. Panels swell and come apart, and a wet cabinet box can keep the wall behind it damp for weeks.

Wall Bases and Trim

On a flooded kitchen or laundry floor, the baseboards and the lower edge of the drywall draw water upward. Often the worst of it is on the wall in back of the appliance, where it went unseen.
