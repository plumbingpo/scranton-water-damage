Sump Pump Failure Cleanup in Scranton: What to Know

A sump pump has one job, and it tends to fail at the moment it is needed most. Heavy rain raises the water table, the pit fills, and then the power goes out, the float sticks or the pump cannot keep up. The pit overflows and the basement floor starts to flood. Until pumping resumes, the water continues to rise.

Do not think of it as clean water. It has come through soil and often carries silt, bacteria and whatever was in the drain tile, so we treat it as Category 2 at best and Category 3 if it has sat or mixed with sewage. That changes the cleanup: porous materials like carpet pad and wet drywall usually come out, and everything left is cleaned and disinfected before drying starts.

Do This First

What to Do Right Now

  1. Keep Out of the BasementNever wade into basement water before the power has been cut. The furnace, wall outlets or the cord of the pump itself could be in contact with it.
  2. Turn Off the Basement CircuitsBasement breakers should be flipped off, provided you can get to the panel without standing in water. A panel surrounded by water is a job for the power company.
  3. Look at the Pump From a DistanceA tripped outlet, a loose plug or a float wedged against the basin wall are the things to look for. Do not reach into the water.
  4. Assume the Water Is DirtyWear rubber boots and gloves if you must go down, keep kids and pets upstairs, and wash your hands after touching anything wet.
  5. Move Valuables and CallCarry dry valuables upstairs, photograph the water level and the pump, then call us. Waiting for it to recede without help is a mistake.
Our Process

Our Sump Pump Failure Cleanup Process

1

Pump Out the Basement

We bring our own pumps and extractors, so nothing depends on your failed sump. We clear silt and standing water off the floor, out of low areas and from around the pit.

2

Take Out What Got Wet

Carpet, padding, insulation and wet drywall that the water contacted are pulled by a crew in protective gear, bagged and removed from the property so the concrete and studs are open.

3

Clean and Sanitize

We wash the framing, the slab and the contents that are staying, then apply an antimicrobial. This happens before drying so contaminants are not blown around the basement.

4

Dry and Confirm

Dehumidifiers and air movers run until moisture meter readings on the framing and concrete return to normal, typically 3 to 5 days. Before we pull our equipment, the pit should have a pump that works.

Coverage

Insurance and Sump Pump Failure Cleanup

Few water claims confuse homeowners as much as this one. A standard homeowners policy usually excludes water that backs up or overflows from a sump, and flood insurance is a separate policy with its own rules. Coverage often exists only if you added a water backup or sump overflow endorsement, and those carry their own limits. Look for that endorsement on your declarations page or ask your agent. Our records of the failed pump, how high the water rose and what it damaged go into your claim file.

The Usual Culprits

Why This Happens

  • A tripped outlet or breaker, or a plug knocked loose, leaving the pump without power.
  • Water returning down the discharge pipe because the check valve no longer seals.
  • Dirt and small rocks washed into the basin that lock up the pump.
  • A discharge line that is blocked, crushed or disconnected outside the house.
  • Motor failure in a pump that has simply reached the end of its life.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Stored Belongings

Books, cloth, boxes and wood furniture legs take in dirty water within a short time. Within days mold shows up on absorbent things that were in it, and they are difficult to disinfect.

Outlets and Wiring

Basement outlets sit low on the wall, where rising water can reach them. Any outlet or wiring that got wet should be checked by an electrician before the circuit is turned back on.

Furnace and Water Heater

Sump pits tend to be located near the home's mechanical equipment. If wiring, burners or control boards got wet they may be ruined, and no appliance should be restarted until it has been checked.

Basement Floor Coverings

There is no safe way to clean pad and carpet that sat in groundwater, so both are removed. Laminate and vinyl plank trap silty water underneath and usually come up too.
