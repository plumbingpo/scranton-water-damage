Ceiling Water Damage Repair: What Scranton Homeowners Should Know

A brown ring on the ceiling is the last thing to show up, not the first. Before any mark shows, water has traveled down a joist, saturated the insulation and collected on top of the drywall. The leak may be a bathroom on the floor above, a pipe in the ceiling, an air conditioner drain line or the roof. Our team finds where it starts before any patching is done.

Painting over the stain does not fix it. Moisture left in the cavity brings the discoloration back and lets mold get going on the paper side in 24 to 48 hours. The opening is kept as small as the damage allows, wet insulation comes out, joists and surrounding drywall are dried, and the area is patched, textured and painted to blend. If the ceiling has started to droop, treat it differently. Do not stand beneath it, and get help on the way.

Signs to Look For

  • Ceiling paint lifting off in blisters or peeling strips
  • Tan or rust colored circles that widen following a storm or a shower
  • Hairline cracks spreading outward from a stained or damp spot
  • Drywall tape lifting and seams or screw heads starting to show
  • Stale, mildew like odor you cannot trace to anything you can see
  • A ceiling panel that droops, bulges or gives under light pressure

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

The first few hours

Hidden Travel Overhead

The insulation gets wet as water travels down the framing. The drywall absorbs it from the back, so the room below may still look fine.

Hours 24 to 48

Marks Start Showing

Discoloration shows up and the paint begins bubbling. Mold can begin on the drywall paper and the wet insulation presses down on the panel.

Day 3 to 7

The Ceiling Bows

Softened gypsum loses its hold on the screws. The ceiling bows between joists, seams open up and a musty odor reaches the room.

Past one week

Risk of Collapse

Waterlogged sections can fall, bringing wet insulation with them. Mold is established in the cavity and the repair now covers a much larger area.

Our Process

How We Handle Ceiling Water Damage Repair

1

Trace the Leak

We track the water back to where it starts using moisture meters and thermal imaging, checking the room or attic above. Repairs are pointless until that leak is shut down.

2

Secure the Room

We cut power to any wet fixture and move or cover the furniture underneath. A ceiling with pooled water is relieved carefully, with the water collected as it comes out.

3

Remove and Dry Out

We take down the ruined drywall and the soaked insulation. Drying equipment, meaning a dehumidifier and air movers, works on the framing and adjacent drywall, with antimicrobial treatment for water that was not clean.

4

Repair and Finish

After the meter shows normal levels, new insulation and drywall go up, joints are taped and finished, the texture is matched and a stain blocking primer goes on ahead of paint.

What You Cannot See

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Damage on the Floor Above

A wet ceiling usually means a wet subfloor or attic deck directly over it. Our inspection includes the space overhead, since moisture left there keeps feeding the problem.

A Ceiling That Lets Go

Water adds a lot of weight to a panel while weakening its hold on the framing. A bulge can drop a whole section onto whatever is underneath.

Wet Lights and Wiring

The damp area usually includes fan mounts, recessed fixtures and wiring boxes. Water in a fixture can short the circuit, so switch off the breaker for that room.

Older Ceiling Materials

Some older popcorn textures and plasters contain asbestos. Testing comes first in such a home, ahead of any cutting, scraping or demolition of the damaged section.
